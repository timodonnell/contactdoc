The main part of the document (... above) is a series of Statements, where each Statement is either a "contact statement" or a "distance".

Distance statements are given as:

<distance> [res1] [res2] [atom1] [atom2] [distance]

Distance statements always give the correct binned distance between two pairs of atoms selected uniformly at random.

Contact statements are given as:

[contact-mode] [res1] [res2]

Where [contact-mode] is one of:

<long-range-contact> -- a long range contact defined as CB (CA for glycine) atoms <= 8 angstroms apart and seq separation >=24
<medium-range-contact> -- same as <long-range-contact> except for sequence distances 12-24
<short-range-contact> -- same as <long-range-contact> except for sequence distances 6-12

Contact statements are only given for residue pairs where CB (CA for glycine) atoms are less than or equal to 8 angstroms apart.

Document structure:
Our token budget is 8192, but some of that is needed prefix with the amino acid sequence and so on. We fill up the remaining capacity as follows. We sample numbers f_long*, f_medium*, and f_short* from a uniform distribution between [-0.1, 0.2] and let f_long = max(0.0, f_long*), f_medium = max(0.0, f_medium*), f_short* = max(0.0, f_short*). 
- We allocate f_long, f_medium, and f_short fraction of our budget equally into long (i.e. mode is <long-range-contact>), medium, and short contacts, respectively. If we run out of contacts for some subset that's ok.
- The remaining tokens are filled with cases where where [mode] is <distance>
- To determine the order, we assign each Statement a rank, where high ranks appear earlier in the document. Statements where [mode] is distance are assigned a rank r ~ N(0, 1), i.e. a rank from a standard normal distribution. Statements where [mode] is <long-range-contact>, <medium-range-contact>, or <short-range-contact> are assigned a rank r ~ N(2.0, 1), i.e. a normal distribution with a higher mean. Thus, the beginning of the document has most of the contact statements.
